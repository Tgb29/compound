No drug class has moved the industry more in the last five years. If you follow one area closely, make it this one.

## The hormone behind it

**GLP-1** is a hormone your gut releases after you eat. It tells your brain you're full, slows down how fast your stomach empties, and helps your pancreas release insulin. The natural version lasts minutes. Drug makers built long-lasting copies — **GLP-1 agonists** — that keep the signal on for a week.

## The first wave

- **Semaglutide** (Novo Nordisk) — sold as **Ozempic** for diabetes since 2017 and **Wegovy** for obesity since 2021. In its main obesity trial, people lost about 15% of their body weight on average over 68 weeks.
- **Tirzepatide** (Eli Lilly) — sold as **Mounjaro** for diabetes and **Zepbound** for obesity. It hits a second hormone receptor, **GIP**, as well as GLP-1, and produced about 21% average weight loss at the highest dose.

Before these, prescription weight-loss drugs typically managed 5–10%.

## It's not only about weight

Trials keep finding benefits beyond the scale. Semaglutide cut the risk of heart attack, stroke and heart-related death by about 20% in people with heart disease. The drugs are now also approved or being tested for sleep apnea, kidney disease, fatty liver disease (**MASH**) and more. That's why some longevity researchers call them the first broad "healthspan" drugs.

## The catches

- **Side effects:** nausea, vomiting and other stomach problems are common, especially when starting.
- **Muscle:** some of the weight lost is muscle, not just fat — a big focus for next-generation drugs.
- **Stopping:** most people regain much of the weight after they stop, so these may be long-term medicines.
- **Cost and supply:** they've been expensive and hard to get, though prices are falling and pills are easier to make at scale.

## What comes next

The race now is for drugs that work better, last longer, or come as a pill. All of these are on the [watchlist](../../watchlist/):

- **Pills.** Novo's **Wegovy pill** (oral semaglutide) launched in the US in January 2026. Lilly's **orforglipron**, sold as **Foundayo**, is a small-molecule pill approved in the US for obesity in April 2026 — easier to manufacture than the protein-based drugs.
- **More hormones at once.** Lilly's **retatrutide** is a **triple agonist**: GLP-1, GIP and glucagon. It produced roughly 21–23% weight loss in two Phase 3 trials, and Lilly plans to file for approval in early 2027.
- **Adding amylin.** Amylin is another fullness hormone. Novo's **CagriSema** pairs it with semaglutide and is waiting on an FDA decision; **amycretin** combines both signals in one molecule.
- **Less frequent shots.** Amgen's **MariTide** is designed to be injected monthly or less.

## Why investors care

Obesity drugs are the biggest-selling drug class in the world, and Lilly and Novo became two of the most valuable healthcare companies on the back of them. Nearly every big drugmaker now wants an obesity program, which is why smaller companies like Viking and Structure get so much attention — and why so many deals in the briefs mention "metabolic" or "cardiometabolic" drugs.
